The problem

First-round interviews don’t scale

They eat hours

Every screening call takes time from HR and from engineers who could be building, often to rule out candidates in the first ten minutes.

Scheduling slows hiring

Getting each candidate into the office, or onto a call at a time that works for both sides, adds days to every hire.

Standards drift

Different interviewers ask different questions and judge answers differently, so candidates aren’t compared like for like.

How it works

From invite link to report, in four steps

  1. 1

    Invite

    The recruiter picks a role and creates an interview link.

  2. 2

    Interview

    The candidate opens the link and talks to the AI interviewer by voice, straight from the browser.

  3. 3

    Adapt

    After each answer the AI decides whether to follow up or move on, and scores the answer against a structured rubric.

  4. 4

    Report

    The recruiter reviews a structured report with per-answer scores and an overall hire signal.

See it in action

Five short clips from a real session

Filmed on a phone pointed at the laptop during a live interview for the Junior AI Engineer role. Turn the sound on: the interview happens by voice.

01 · 0:18

Before the first question

The interviewer speaks six short setup lines (quiet room, headphones, stable connection, “you may interrupt me”) and shows each one as it is said. The candidate clicks Ready to begin.

02 · 0:17

The opening question

The session connects and the interviewer greets the candidate by name for the role, then asks them to introduce themselves and describe one project they’re proud of.

03 · 0:31

A follow-up built from the answer

The candidate mentions a news classifier built on a fine-tuned BERT model. The next question picks up exactly that: which metrics did they use to evaluate it?

04 · 0:50

When the candidate says “I don’t know”

Asked how they’d handle data versioning across their modules, the candidate says they don’t know. The interviewer doesn’t score a blank answer. It gives a short hint and asks again.

05 · 0:29

Interruptions, handled like a person would

With sound on, you’ll hear the candidate talk over the interviewer during a question about overfitting. The interviewer replies “Happy to repeat” and asks the question again, more simply.

For recruiters

A dashboard, not another inbox

Recruiters sign in to a separate dashboard to send interviews out and review the results when they’re ready.

  • Invite links. Create a link for a role (Junior AI Engineer, Frontend or Backend Developer) and share it however you like.
  • Optional email. Send the invitation straight from the dashboard when email is set up. Copying the link always works.
  • Per-answer scores. Each answer is scored 1–5 on a six-part competency rubric: structure, result orientation, ownership, leadership, clarity and confidence. Every score sits next to the question and the answer it came from.
  • A hire signal that supports your call. Strong Hire, Hire, Borderline or No Hire, labelled in the report as decision support, not a final hiring decision. If an interview ends early, the report says so and withholds the overall signal.
